Cinematy · Frame by Frame02 / 05
1927 · The talkies arrive
It was a money decision.
Silent films were cranked by hand, drifting anywhere from 16 to 20 frames a second. Then came sound, and sound only plays right at one steady speed.
Studios needed a number fast enough for clear sound and slow enough to save film. 24 won. A century later, it’s still winning.

Cinematy · Frame by Frame03 / 05
Why 24 still feels like cinema
The blur is the magic.
At 24 fps, each frame is exposed for 1/48 of a second. Anything moving leaves a soft smear. That tiny imperfection is the difference between a dream and a security camera.
24 fps · 1/48 s
120 fps · 1/240 s
Soft. Dreamy. Cinema.
Sharp. Clinical. Live TV.

Cinematy · Frame by Frame04 / 05
So why not more frames?
Hollywood tried to upgrade.
Too real
Decades of live TV taught us smooth = real. Suddenly sets look like sets.
Too expensive
Double the frames, double the data, the VFX renders and the light.
Where more frames do win: slow-mo. Shoot 120, play at 24, and one second lasts five.
